Why ranking changed away from ipTM

The Smorodina benchmark gives 91 real cognate nanobody–antigen pairs on the diagonal of a 91×91 matrix and roughly 8,100 shuffled pairings off it, scored by the same models we use. That makes the filter testable.

At the conventional 0.8 cutoff Boltz-2 admits 81.7% of known non-binders against 90.1% of real pairs: 1.10× enrichment, effectively no information. Its usable cutoff is 0.951, where it keeps under a fifth of true binders. Chai-1's 89× is a trap — it keeps 1.1% of real pairs, meaning one. Adaptyv's EGFR results put the same metrics at ~0.2 correlation with measured outcome, which is the same conclusion from real experiments rather than shuffled pairs.

What the refold check caught

The de novo VHH track was dropped on measured evidence

100 de novo VHHs were generated and the top 20 refolded. Every one failed. The nanobodies fold well on their own — binder_ptm 0.95–0.99, binder_plddt 89–94 — but Boltz-2 places them 10.9 to 55.2 Å from the designed pose. They are well-formed domains that bind somewhere else.

Two alternative explanations were ruled out by measurement. The target is not at fault: superposing each refold on the template gives chain C a self-RMSD of 0.45 Å, chain D displaced 2.0 Å, and the C572–D591 gap preserved at 15.8 Å against 16.25 Å designed — so splitting HER2 domain IV across two chains did no harm. And the metrics contradict each other, which is the decisive tell: the design with the best interface PAE (2.48) and best ipTM (0.82) sits 39 Å from its designed pose, while the best-docked design (10.9 Å) has an interface PAE of 18.2. No design is good on both, so none looks like a real binder.

One explanation is still open, and it bounds the conclusion: the refold ran with msa_path: None on every chain, so Boltz-2 predicted single-sequence. It is an MSA-dependent model, so its pose judgement was handicapped and 0/20 overstates how bad the designs are. AF2-IG is the right control — it starts from the designed coordinates instead of rediscovering the pose from sequence. Until that runs, treat this as probable, not settled.

Root cause at the level of the weights: de novo ran on antibody-2026-10-30, the general antibody checkpoint, which knows nothing about HER2. Both HER2-finetuned checkpoints only redesign CDRH3 on a fixed framework, so the de novo track could not use the one asset actually trained on this target.

Every design we have passed through one design model and one folding model, and the calibration above says that folding model is the weakest of the three measured. A second opinion from a different architecture would separate "the designs are bad" from "Boltz-2 is wrong about them" — right now those are indistinguishable.

Open gaps

  • Thermostability has no score of any kind, and it is a third of the result. The 130 liability occurrences abguard already reports are free input.
  • epitope_fraction is computed for all 100 designs and joined to nothing. 37 of 100 fall below 1.0, so it discriminates — though on the refolded set it did not predict docking success, so treat it as a measurement of the judged axis rather than a binding predictor.
  • Two ranking paths still exist: evaluate.py computes a composite that submission.py ignores. One of them should go.
  • Interface size is the one cheap signal that tracked docking accuracy on the refolded set (contacts ρ = −0.58, H-bond pairs ρ = −0.44, and longer VHH worse at ρ = +0.50) — all at n = 20, so suggestive and not settled.
